# UP-2: list calls translated in the queue's line (LEONIDA_THIN)

`LEONIDA_THIN=1` (off; after Belobug/VIctory-Belobug-AMD 3a99ee6,
`list/thin.rs`): list calls only write down their arguments, and threads
of ours (`LEONIDA_THIN_THREADS`, 8) translate them onto lists of ours,
beginning while the title still records. Translating inside the calls
took 300 to 350 ms of the recording threads' time a frame in the first
rooms (RTX 3080, 2026-11-26).

With `LEONIDA_ASYNC_SUBMIT=1` (in `base`) `ExecuteCommandLists` puts the
lists' recordings in the queue's line (`Held::Lists`) and returns. The
line stands at them until every one is translated, forced or not; then
the parts of the lists of ours take their place, as a submission cut at
its waits would have had them, ahead of everything behind them. The end
of a translation wakes the submit thread. What must not pass a
submission waits for its translation instead: a submission of ours that
is waited for (`before_sync`), a present before it settles the flights
or reads the shown texture's state, and a forced present (only for
submissions made before it began, so other title threads cannot keep
it there). Without the async submit the call waits for the translation
itself; on an RTX 3080 12 GB (driver 621.24) that lost the device, with
a median frame of 1,569 ms and 20.5 s of waiting per 4,096 submissions.

Log lines: "lists translated by threads of ours" and "submissions
translated in their queue's line" (how many the line stood at, and how
long presents waited for translations).

## Measured

RTX 3080 12 GB, driver 621.24, Ryzen 7 7800X3D, 2026-11-27: four runs of
`scripts/measure-run.ps1` on the diagnostics build in one series, off and
`LEONIDA_THIN=1` taking turns. Milliseconds a frame:

| run | median frame | usual frame | card | preparing | copying back |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| off | 143.5 | 134 | 36.4 | 73.7 | 29.4 |
| on | 147 | 142.5 | 42.6 | 67.6 | 30 |
| off | 142.5 | 134 | 44 | 71.1 | 29.6 |
| on | 152.5 | 145.5 | 47.4 | 67.1 | 28.2 |

No run lost the device. Preparing is 4 to 6 ms shorter with the switch,
but the frame is 4 to 10 ms longer on this card; why the card's time
grows is not known. All four runs stayed in the opening's cutscenes, so
play is not measured. Other cards and processors are not measured: a
processor with fewer fast cores, where the recording threads wait more,
may gain from it (a guess).
